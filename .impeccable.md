## Design Context

### Users
Primary users are small business owners with limited design vocabulary and limited confidence in what kind of post they should create. The system should help them externalize tacit brand knowledge, refine fuzzy goals into usable direction, and understand why the system is suggesting a given visual path.

### Brand Personality
The interface should feel clean, minimalistic, and actually usable. It should support a premium editorial tone rather than a flashy experimental tool. The product should feel like a careful creative assistant for serious business owners, not a toy.

### Aesthetic Direction
Use light mode only for now. Favor restrained, polished, editorial composition with clear structure and legible hierarchy. Preserve useful parts of the current workshop demo, but avoid any look that feels obviously vibe-coded, template-generated, or generically AI-made.

### Design Principles
1. Make tacit intent legible: help users move from vague feelings to concrete post goals without forcing design jargon too early.
2. Keep the interface editorial and calm: use whitespace, hierarchy, and disciplined composition instead of loud decoration.
3. Show reasoning and memory: users should be able to see how feedback was interpreted, how preferences evolved, and why a suggestion exists.
4. Prefer usability over cleverness: every interaction should feel deliberate, understandable, and easy to continue from.
5. Preserve continuity while refining: design updates should clean up the system without introducing a conflicting second visual language.
